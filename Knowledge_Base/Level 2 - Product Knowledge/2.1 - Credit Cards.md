# 4. Product Overview

## 4.1 Introduction

A Credit Card is an unsecured revolving line of credit issued by HDFC Bank that enables eligible customers to purchase goods and services without making immediate payment from their own deposit accounts.

Unlike a Debit Card, which directly accesses funds available in a customer's bank account, a Credit Card allows the customer to borrow funds up to an approved credit limit and repay the borrowed amount at a later date according to an agreed billing cycle.

The product is designed to provide convenience, purchasing power, financial flexibility, digital payment capabilities, and access to value-added benefits such as rewards, cashback, travel privileges, merchant offers, and installment payment options.

Credit Cards represent one of the most widely used retail lending products within the bank and form an important component of the broader consumer banking ecosystem.

---

## 4.2 Product Definition

A HDFC Bank Credit Card represents an approved revolving credit facility linked to an individual customer relationship.

Each Credit Card is associated with:

* A Credit Card Account
* An approved Credit Limit
* A Billing Cycle
* A Repayment Schedule
* A Card Network
* A Customer Relationship
* Risk and Servicing Controls

The customer may repeatedly utilise available credit and replenish it through repayments without applying for a new loan each time.

This revolving nature differentiates Credit Cards from traditional installment-based lending products.

# 5. Credit Card Fundamentals

## 5.1 Introduction

Understanding the fundamental concepts of Credit Cards is essential before examining product features, servicing journeys, operational workflows, or customer interactions.

These concepts define how credit is extended, utilised, repaid, monitored, and managed throughout the lifecycle of the product.

This section establishes the foundational vocabulary that AI systems, service agents, workflow engines, and downstream applications should consistently interpret.

---

## 5.2 What is Credit?

Credit is the ability to borrow money from a financial institution with an obligation to repay it in the future under agreed terms and conditions.

The bank extends credit based on its assessment of:

* Creditworthiness
* Repayment capacity
* Financial profile
* Existing obligations
* Risk appetite

Unlike deposits, which represent customer-owned funds, credit represents money owed to the bank.

---

## 5.3 Revolving Credit

Credit Cards operate on a revolving credit model.

Unlike traditional loans where a fixed amount is borrowed and repaid over a predetermined schedule, revolving credit allows customers to:

* Borrow repeatedly
* Repay partially or fully
* Reuse available credit after repayment
* Continue utilising the facility without reapplying

This revolving mechanism is one of the defining characteristics of Credit Card products.

---

## 5.4 Credit Limit

The Credit Limit represents the maximum amount that a customer is permitted to borrow through the Credit Card Account at any given point in time.

The approved limit is determined using factors such as:

* Income
* Employment profile
* Credit history
* Existing liabilities
* Banking relationship
* Internal risk assessments

The available portion of the limit changes dynamically based on customer activity.

---

### Example

```text
Approved Credit Limit: ₹200,000

Outstanding Usage: ₹50,000

Available Credit: ₹150,000
```

---

## 5.5 Available Credit

Available Credit refers to the unused portion of the approved Credit Limit that remains available for future transactions.

Available Credit may change due to:

* Purchases
* Cash Advances
* EMI conversions
* Payments
* Refunds
* Reversals
* Limit enhancements

Available Credit is dynamic and continuously updated as account activity occurs.

---

## 5.6 Billing Cycle

A Billing Cycle is the recurring period during which eligible transactions are accumulated for statement generation.

At the end of the cycle, the Billing Engine generates:

* Statement Balance
* Outstanding Balance
* Minimum Amount Due
* Payment Due Date
* Interest calculations
* Applicable fees and charges

The Billing Cycle forms the foundation of the repayment process.

---

## 5.7 Statement

A Credit Card Statement is a periodic summary of account activity during a Billing Cycle.

Statements typically contain:

* Purchases
* Refunds
* Reversals
* Payments received
* Interest charges
* Fees
* Taxes
* Reward summaries
* Outstanding balances
* Due dates

Statements provide customers with a consolidated view of account activity.

---

## 5.8 Outstanding Balance

Outstanding Balance represents the total amount currently owed by the customer to the bank.

It may include:

* Purchases
* EMIs
* Fees
* Interest
* Taxes
* Cash Advances

The Outstanding Balance changes continuously as transactions and repayments occur.

---

## 5.9 Payment Due Date

The Payment Due Date is the final date by which the customer is expected to make payment for the statemented balance.

Failure to make payment by the due date may result in:

* Interest charges
* Late payment fees
* Delinquency reporting
* Collections activity

---

## 5.10 Interest-Free Period

Eligible retail purchases may receive an Interest-Free Period when the statement balance is paid in full by the due date.

The actual interest-free duration depends on:

* Transaction date
* Statement date
* Billing cycle configuration
* Repayment behaviour

The Interest-Free Period is one of the primary benefits associated with Credit Card usage.

---

## 5.11 Minimum Amount Due (MAD)

The Minimum Amount Due (MAD) represents the minimum payment required to keep the account from being considered unpaid for a billing cycle.

The MAD is generally only a fraction of the total outstanding balance.

Paying only the MAD:

* May prevent immediate delinquency classification
* Does not necessarily eliminate finance charges
* May result in continued revolving balances

Customers who consistently pay only the MAD may accumulate long-term debt obligations.

---

## 5.12 Partial Payment

Customers are not always required to repay the full statement balance.

Possible repayment behaviours include:

* Full Payment
* Partial Payment
* Minimum Amount Due Payment

Partial repayment may reduce outstanding balances while still resulting in finance charges on the remaining unpaid amount.

---

## 5.13 Finance Charges

Finance Charges represent the cost of borrowing when eligible balances are not repaid according to product terms.

Finance Charges may apply to:

* Revolving balances
* Cash Advances
* Certain fee categories

Calculation methodologies are governed by product terms and applicable regulations.

---

## 5.14 Cash Advance

A Cash Advance allows customers to withdraw cash using a portion of their approved Credit Limit.

Cash Advances are generally subject to:

* Separate limits
* Additional fees
* Immediate finance charges
* Different repayment treatment

Cash Advances should not be interpreted as standard purchase transactions.

---

## 5.15 Credit Utilisation

Credit Utilisation measures how much of the approved Credit Limit is currently being used.

It is generally expressed as a percentage.

### Formula

```text
Credit Utilisation (%) =
Outstanding Balance ÷ Credit Limit × 100
```

### Example

```text
Credit Limit: ₹100,000

Outstanding Balance: ₹30,000

Utilisation: 30%
```

High utilisation levels may influence a customer's overall credit profile.

---

## 5.16 Rewards

Many Credit Cards include rewards programmes that incentivise customer spending.

Common reward mechanisms include:

* Reward Points
* Cashback
* Airline Miles
* Travel Credits
* Merchant Discounts
* Lifestyle Benefits
* Accelerated Earning Categories

Reward eligibility depends on:

* Product variant
* Transaction type
* Merchant category
* Campaign rules

---

## 5.17 EMI Conversion

Eligible transactions may be converted into Equated Monthly Instalments (EMIs).

EMI conversion allows customers to:

* Spread repayments across multiple months
* Convert large purchases into structured repayments
* Manage cash flow more effectively

EMI eligibility depends on product rules and transaction characteristics.

---

## 5.18 Security Concepts

Modern Credit Cards incorporate multiple security controls.

Common security mechanisms include:

### EMV Chip

Protects against card cloning and counterfeit fraud.

---

### PIN Authentication

Validates customer identity during card-present transactions.

---

### CVV

Additional authentication factor for online transactions.

---

### OTP Authentication

Provides transaction-specific verification.

---

### Tokenization

Replaces sensitive card details with secure digital tokens.

---

### Fraud Monitoring

Continuously evaluates transaction behaviour for suspicious activity.

---

## 5.19 Delinquency

Delinquency occurs when required payments are not received within expected timelines.

Progressive delinquency may result in:

* Interest accumulation
* Late fees
* Collections activities
* Credit bureau reporting
* Account restrictions

Delinquency is a credit risk concept rather than a servicing request.

---

## 5.20 Customer Liability

Customers are generally responsible for:

* Repayment obligations
* Transaction verification
* Protecting credentials
* Reporting suspicious activity
* Complying with product terms

Liability treatment may vary depending on fraud scenarios, regulatory requirements, and dispute outcomes.

---

## AI Interpretation Notes

AI systems should understand the following principles:

* A Credit Card is a revolving credit product rather than a deposit product.
* Credit Limit and Available Credit are different concepts.
* Paying the Minimum Amount Due does not necessarily eliminate finance charges.
* Cash Advances are distinct from retail purchases.
* Credit Utilisation is a risk indicator rather than a fee.
* Statements summarise account activity but do not create transactions.
* Rewards, EMI eligibility, and benefits vary by product variant.
* Delinquency is related to repayment behaviour and should not be confused with servicing issues.
* Security controls operate independently of billing and repayment concepts.

## 6.3 Architectural Principles

The Credit Card ecosystem operates according to several foundational principles.

### Principle 1 – Customer Owns the Relationship

The customer owns the banking relationship.

Products exist within the context of that relationship.

---

### Principle 2 – Accounts Own Financial Activity

Transactions, balances, limits, statements, and repayments belong to the Credit Card Account.

---

### Principle 3 – Cards Provide Access

Physical and digital cards provide access to the account but are not the account itself.

---

### Principle 4 – Networks Enable Acceptance

Card Networks facilitate payment processing but do not extend credit.

---

### Principle 5 – Servicing Does Not Own Financial Data

Servicing platforms orchestrate customer requests but do not act as the system of record for financial balances.

# 7. Card Networks & Payment Ecosystem

## 7.1 Purpose

Every HDFC Bank Credit Card operates on an underlying payment network.

The payment network acts as the intermediary that enables transactions between customers, merchants, acquiring institutions, issuing banks, and payment processors.

Without a payment network, a Credit Card cannot be accepted across merchant locations or digital payment channels.

Understanding the payment ecosystem is essential because many customer servicing requests originate from transaction-related events, including:

* Transaction failures
* Duplicate charges
* Refunds
* Reversals
* Chargebacks
* International transactions
* Merchant disputes

AI systems should understand how funds, messages, and authorization decisions move through the payment ecosystem before attempting to interpret transaction-related requests.

---

## 7.2 What is a Card Network?

A Card Network is a payment infrastructure that facilitates electronic payment transactions between participating entities.

The network provides:

* Merchant acceptance
* Authorization routing
* Clearing
* Settlement
* Security standards
* Operating regulations
* International interoperability

The network does not extend credit.

Credit is always provided by the issuing bank.

---

## 7.3 Role of HDFC Bank

Within the payment ecosystem, HDFC Bank primarily functions as the Issuing Bank (Issuer).

As the issuer, HDFC Bank is responsible for:

* Customer onboarding
* Credit approval
* Credit limit assignment
* Card issuance
* Authorization decisions
* Billing
* Collections
* Rewards management
* Customer servicing
* Risk management

The bank owns the credit relationship with the customer.

---

## 7.4 Key Participants in the Payment Ecosystem

A typical Credit Card transaction involves multiple participants.

### Customer

The individual initiating the transaction.

The customer:

* Uses the Credit Card
* Receives credit
* Makes repayments
* Initiates disputes when required

---

### Merchant

The merchant provides goods or services in exchange for payment.

Merchants may include:

* Retail stores
* Restaurants
* Hotels
* E-commerce platforms
* Utility providers
* Subscription services

---

### Acquiring Bank (Acquirer)

The Acquiring Bank provides payment acceptance services to merchants.

Responsibilities include:

* Merchant onboarding
* Transaction acceptance
* Payment processing
* Merchant settlement

The Acquirer represents the merchant within the payment ecosystem.

---

### Card Network

The Card Network acts as the communication and settlement layer between the Acquirer and Issuer.

Responsibilities include:

* Routing authorization requests
* Clearing transactions
* Settlement coordination
* Security standards
* Dispute framework governance

---

### Issuing Bank (Issuer)

The Issuer provides the Credit Card and extends credit to the customer.

For HDFC Bank Credit Cards, HDFC Bank acts as the Issuer.

---

## 7.8 Authorization

Authorization occurs when the merchant requests approval for a transaction.

The authorization request is routed through:

```text id="txf002"
Merchant

↓

Acquirer

↓

Card Network

↓

Issuer (HDFC Bank)
```

The Issuer evaluates:

* Available Credit
* Card Status
* Authentication Results
* Fraud Controls
* Risk Rules
* Transaction Restrictions

The Issuer then returns an approval or decline decision.

---

## 7.9 Clearing

Clearing occurs after authorization.

During Clearing:

* Transaction records are exchanged
* Merchant details are validated
* Settlement information is prepared
* Network obligations are calculated

Clearing is an intermediary stage between authorization and settlement.

---

## 7.10 Settlement

Settlement is the process through which financial obligations are transferred between institutions.

Settlement involves:

* Issuer
* Network
* Acquirer
* Merchant

Settlement ensures that merchants ultimately receive payment for approved transactions.

---

## 7.11 Domestic Transactions

Domestic Transactions occur when:

* Card issued in India
* Merchant located in India
* Settlement occurs within the domestic ecosystem

Domestic transactions generally involve:

* Local currency processing
* Domestic regulatory controls
* Network-specific routing

---

## 7.12 International Transactions

International Transactions occur when:

* Merchant is located outside India
* Foreign currency conversion is required
* Cross-border settlement is involved

Additional considerations may include:

* Foreign exchange conversion
* International transaction fees
* Cross-border compliance requirements
* Enhanced risk controls

The overall transaction lifecycle remains similar to domestic transactions.

---

## 7.13 Contactless Payments

Contactless payments allow customers to complete eligible transactions by tapping the card near a supported payment terminal.

Benefits include:

* Faster transactions
* Reduced physical contact
* Convenience
* Improved customer experience

Contactless transactions continue to use the same underlying payment network infrastructure.

---

## 7.14 Tokenization

Tokenization replaces sensitive card details with a secure digital token.

Benefits include:

* Reduced exposure of card numbers
* Improved digital security
* Safer mobile wallet transactions
* Reduced fraud risk

The token represents the underlying card without exposing actual card credentials.

---

## 7.15 Security Standards

Card Networks establish industry-wide security standards.

Examples include:

* EMV Standards
* PCI DSS Requirements
* Tokenization Frameworks
* Contactless Protocols
* Authentication Standards
* Fraud Monitoring Guidelines

Issuing banks implement these standards within their products and systems.

---

## 7.16 Disputes & Chargebacks

Card Networks define dispute and chargeback frameworks.

These frameworks govern situations such as:

* Fraudulent transactions
* Duplicate charges
* Incorrect transaction amounts
* Goods not received
* Service not delivered

Dispute resolution typically involves:

* Customer
* Issuer
* Card Network
* Acquirer
* Merchant

Network rules govern the resolution process.

---

## 7.17 Ecosystem Responsibilities

The following table summarises participant responsibilities.

| Participant        | Primary Responsibility                    |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Customer           | Initiates transaction and repayment       |
| Merchant           | Provides goods or services                |
| Acquirer           | Merchant payment acceptance               |
| Card Network       | Transaction routing and settlement        |
| HDFC Bank (Issuer) | Credit, authorization, billing, servicing |

---

## AI Interpretation Notes

AI systems should understand that:

* HDFC Bank issues the credit.
* Card Networks provide payment infrastructure.
* Card Networks do not lend money.
* Merchant acceptance depends on network participation.
* Authorization is different from settlement.
* Domestic and international transactions follow similar transaction principles.
* Disputes are governed by network rules.
* Security controls are jointly enforced by networks and issuers.
* Most transaction-related servicing journeys require understanding which participant is responsible for the reported issue.

# 9. Credit Card Lifecycle

---

## Purpose

A Credit Card is not a static banking product. It progresses through a well-defined lifecycle that begins before the customer receives the card and continues until the credit relationship is formally terminated.

Each stage of the lifecycle introduces new business processes, customer interactions, servicing requirements, risk assessments, and operational events.

Understanding this lifecycle enables AI systems to correctly identify:

- Where the customer currently exists in the lifecycle
- Which actions are permissible
- Which downstream systems may be involved
- What servicing requests are valid
- What business rules may apply

This lifecycle represents the conceptual evolution of a credit card account rather than the operational workflow executed by individual systems.

---

## Lifecycle Overview

The HDFC Bank Credit Card lifecycle can be represented as follows:

```text
Prospect
   │
   ▼
Application
   │
   ▼
Verification
   │
   ▼
Credit Assessment
   │
   ▼
Approval / Rejection
   │
   ▼
Account Creation
   │
   ▼
Card Issuance
   │
   ▼
Dispatch & Delivery
   │
   ▼
Activation
   │
   ▼
Active Usage
   │
   ▼
Billing & Repayment
   │
   ▼
Ongoing Servicing
   │
   ▼
Upgrade / Renewal
   │
   ▼
Suspension (If Applicable)
   │
   ▼
Closure
```

---

## Lifecycle Design Principles

Several principles remain true throughout the lifecycle.

### Principle 1 — Every Card Belongs to an Account

A physical or virtual card always exists against an underlying credit card account.

The account is the financial entity.

The card is the payment instrument.

---

### Principle 2 — Every Account Belongs to a Customer

A credit card account cannot exist independently.

Every account must be associated with a customer relationship.

---

### Principle 3 — Cards May Change

Physical cards may be:

- Renewed
- Replaced
- Upgraded
- Reissued

The underlying account often remains unchanged.

---

### Principle 4 — Servicing Occurs During Active Relationship

Customer servicing activities generally occur after activation and before closure.

Examples include:

- PIN reset
- Card block
- Replacement
- EMI conversion
- Statement requests

---

### Principle 5 — Closure Terminates the Relationship

Once closure is completed, the customer no longer possesses an active credit card relationship for that account.

# 10. Credit Limits, Exposure & Utilisation

---

## Purpose

Credit Limits form the foundation of the Credit Card product.

Every HDFC Bank Credit Card operates within a predefined borrowing capacity approved by the bank. This capacity governs how much credit a customer may access, how transactions are authorised, how risk is managed, and how customer behaviour is evaluated over time.

Understanding credit limits, exposure, utilisation, and related concepts is critical for AI systems because many servicing requests, transaction outcomes, customer queries, and risk controls are directly influenced by limit availability.

This section establishes the conceptual framework for understanding how credit capacity is assigned, consumed, replenished, and managed throughout the lifecycle of a credit card relationship.

---

## Introduction to Credit Limits

A Credit Limit represents the maximum amount of revolving credit that the bank permits a customer to utilise at a given point in time.

The limit acts as a borrowing boundary that controls the customer's spending capacity.

Credit limits are established based on factors such as:

- Income
- Employment profile
- Existing liabilities
- Credit history
- Internal risk assessment
- Banking relationship
- Portfolio strategy

The approved limit is associated with the credit card account rather than the physical card itself.

---

## Credit Limit Architecture

A simplified view of credit limit management is shown below.

```text
Approved Credit Limit
          │
          ▼
 Available Credit
          │
          ▼
 Customer Transactions
          │
          ▼
 Outstanding Balance
          │
          ▼
 Repayment
          │
          ▼
 Credit Replenishment
```

The available credit continuously changes as transactions and repayments occur.

---

## Types of Credit Limits

### Total Credit Limit

The Total Credit Limit represents the maximum approved borrowing capacity associated with a credit card account.

Example:

```text
Approved Credit Limit = ₹5,00,000
```

The customer cannot ordinarily exceed this amount through approved transactions.

---

### Available Credit Limit

Available Credit represents the unused portion of the approved credit limit.

Example:

```text
Total Limit       = ₹5,00,000
Outstanding Usage = ₹1,50,000

Available Credit  = ₹3,50,000
```

This value changes dynamically throughout the billing cycle.

---

### Utilised Credit

Utilised Credit represents the amount currently consumed by:

- Purchases
- EMIs
- Cash advances
- Pending settlements
- Other eligible account activity

---

### Cash Withdrawal Limit

Many credit cards allocate a separate limit for ATM cash withdrawals.

Characteristics may include:

- Lower than total credit limit
- Separate controls
- Additional fees
- Different interest treatment

The cash withdrawal limit should not be confused with the overall credit limit.

---

### International Usage Limits

Certain transaction categories may be governed by additional controls.

Examples include:

- International spending limits
- Contactless limits
- Merchant category controls
- Channel-specific limits

These controls operate alongside the primary credit limit.

---

## Shared vs Individual Limits

### Individual Credit Limit

An individual limit applies to a standalone credit card account.

The entire approved limit belongs to that specific account relationship.

---

### Shared Credit Limit

Where add-on cards exist, multiple cards may consume the same underlying credit limit.

Example:

```text
Primary Card
      │
      ▼
Credit Limit ₹5,00,000
      │
 ┌────┴────┐
 ▼         ▼
Primary   Add-on
Card       Card
```

Transactions performed using any linked card affect the same available limit.

---

## Credit Utilisation

### Definition

Credit Utilisation measures how much of the approved credit limit is currently being used.

It is generally expressed as a percentage.

Formula:

```text
Credit Utilisation =
(Outstanding Balance ÷ Total Credit Limit) × 100
```

---

### Example

```text
Credit Limit       = ₹2,00,000
Outstanding Usage  = ₹60,000

Utilisation = 30%
```

---

### Business Importance

Credit Utilisation is a significant indicator of customer behaviour.

It may influence:

- Risk assessment
- Credit reviews
- Limit enhancement decisions
- Credit bureau reporting
- Portfolio management

---

## Credit Exposure

### Definition

Credit Exposure represents the bank's financial risk associated with a customer's outstanding obligations.

Exposure may include:

- Retail purchases
- Cash advances
- EMIs
- Interest charges
- Fees
- Pending settlements

---

### Exposure Monitoring

Banks continuously monitor exposure levels to ensure:

- Responsible lending
- Risk management
- Regulatory compliance
- Portfolio quality

Exposure management is therefore a core risk function.

---

## Credit Limit Consumption Events

The following events typically reduce available credit.

### Purchases

Retail and online purchases consume available credit immediately upon authorisation or settlement.

---

### Cash Advances

ATM withdrawals consume credit capacity and may trigger separate fee structures.

---

### EMI Conversions

Converted transactions continue to occupy a portion of the approved credit limit until repaid.

---

### Fees & Charges

Applicable fees may contribute to utilised credit.

Examples include:

- Annual fees
- Late payment charges
- Finance charges
- Service fees

---

### Interest Charges

Interest applied to revolving balances may also affect overall exposure.

---

## Credit Replenishment Events

The following activities generally restore available credit.

### Customer Repayments

Payments made by customers increase available credit.

---

### Transaction Reversals

Authorisations that are reversed release previously reserved credit.

---

### Merchant Refunds

Refunds may restore credit availability after processing.

---

### Chargeback Resolution

Successful dispute resolution may reduce outstanding exposure.

---

## Limit Enhancements

### Definition

A Limit Enhancement increases the approved credit capacity associated with an account.

---

### Possible Drivers

Enhancements may occur due to:

- Improved income profile
- Strong repayment behaviour
- Internal bank review
- Customer request
- Portfolio campaigns
- Relationship value

---

### Outcome

Example:

```text
Previous Limit = ₹3,00,000

Enhanced Limit = ₹5,00,000
```

The available borrowing capacity increases accordingly.

---

## Limit Reductions

### Definition

A Limit Reduction decreases the approved borrowing capacity.

---

### Possible Drivers

Examples include:

- Risk reassessment
- Portfolio strategy changes
- Regulatory requirements
- Customer request
- Credit profile deterioration

---

### Outcome

The customer's future borrowing capacity is reduced.

---

## Overlimit Situations

### Definition

An Overlimit situation occurs when account exposure exceeds the approved credit limit.

---

### Causes

Potential causes include:

- Fees posted after limit exhaustion
- Interest accrual
- Delayed settlements
- Exceptional account activity

---

### Business Handling

Treatment depends on applicable business rules and bank policies.

AI systems should not assume that all overlimit situations are handled identically.

---

## Relationship with Other Credit Products

Credit limit management exists across multiple banking products.

Examples include:

- Personal Loans
- Overdraft Facilities
- Loan Against Securities
- Business Credit Facilities

However, Credit Cards differ because they operate using a revolving limit structure.

---

## AI Interpretation Notes

AI systems should understand that:

- The credit limit belongs to the account, not the physical card.
- Available credit changes continuously.
- Add-on cards may consume the same shared limit.
- Credit utilisation and credit exposure are related but distinct concepts.
- EMI transactions continue to occupy available credit.
- Repayments replenish borrowing capacity.

- Credit limits are central to transaction approval, risk management, and customer servicing decisions.

## Purpose

Billing, Statements, and Repayments form the financial servicing backbone of the Credit Card ecosystem.

- Outstanding balances
- Payment status
- Due dates
- Interest charges
- Minimum Amount Due
- Repayment options
- Billing disputes

This section establishes the foundational concepts required for understanding how financial obligations are generated and settled within the HDFC Bank Credit Card ecosystem.

## Billing Overview

Billing is the process through which the bank periodically calculates and communicates a customer's financial obligations arising from credit card usage.

At regular intervals, the billing system:

- Consolidates account activity
- Calculates outstanding balances
- Applies applicable fees and charges
- Determines repayment obligations
- Generates statements
- Assigns payment due dates

Billing operates continuously throughout the active lifecycle of a credit card account.

Every transaction progresses through a lifecycle before becoming part of the customer's account history.

Transactions performed through digital channels.

### Reversal Transactions

Transactions that negate or cancel a previously initiated transaction.

- Processing corrections

### Adjustment Transactions

Administrative account corrections applied by authorised systems or business processes.

- Fee reversals

- Dispute resolutions

# 17. AI Guardrails & Response Principles

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this section is to define the behavioural boundaries, safety principles, compliance constraints, and response standards that AI systems must follow when interacting with customers regarding Credit Card products.

While previous sections describe product knowledge, lifecycle understanding, and interpretation frameworks, this section defines how AI should behave when applying that knowledge in customer-facing or employee-facing interactions.

These guardrails ensure that AI systems remain accurate, compliant, explainable, trustworthy, and aligned with enterprise banking policies.

## Why AI Guardrails Are Required

Credit Cards are regulated financial products involving:

- Customer funds
- Borrowed money
- Credit exposure
- Personal information
- Financial risk
- Regulatory obligations

Incorrect guidance may result in:

- Financial loss
- Customer dissatisfaction
- Compliance violations
- Operational errors
- Reputational damage

Therefore, AI systems must operate within clearly defined boundaries.

---

## Core Guardrail Principles

All Credit Card AI systems should adhere to the following principles.

### Accuracy

Responses should be based on approved knowledge sources.

AI should:

- Use enterprise knowledge assets
- Use approved product information
- Avoid assumptions
- Avoid fabricated details

AI must never invent:

- Product features
- Fees
- Benefits
- Eligibility criteria
- Approval outcomes

---

### Transparency

AI should clearly distinguish between:

- Known information
- Customer-specific information
- Assumptions
- Recommendations

Customers should understand:

- Why a recommendation is made
- What information is being used
- What information is unavailable

---

### Explainability

AI recommendations should be explainable.

Example:

```text
Recommended:
Travel Credit Card

Reason:
Customer primarily spends on travel and airline bookings.
```

The explanation should be understandable to non-technical users.

---

### Consistency

Equivalent requests should generate equivalent interpretations.

Example:

```text
Lost my card.

I misplaced my card.

I cannot find my card.
```

All should map to:

```text
Intent = Card Loss
Capability = Card Block / Replacement
```

---

### Customer-Centricity

AI should prioritize customer needs over product promotion.

Recommendations should focus on:

- Suitability
- Relevance
- Customer value

rather than sales optimization alone.

AI-generated card recommendations require additional controls.

---

### Suitable Product Principle

- Banking relationship

# 19. Credit Card Business Glossary & Terminology

---

## Purpose

The purpose of this section is to establish a standardized vocabulary for Credit Card-related concepts used across customer interactions, servicing operations, product discussions, AI systems, workflow engines, and enterprise knowledge assets.

A common terminology framework ensures that:

- Business teams use consistent language.
- AI systems interpret concepts correctly.
- Service agents understand customer intent accurately.
- Product knowledge remains standardized across channels.
- Ambiguity is minimized during servicing and decision-making.

This glossary should serve as the canonical terminology reference for all Credit Card-related knowledge within the Knowledge Engine.

---

## Why a Business Glossary is Required

Customers, agents, product teams, and systems often use different terms to describe the same concept.

For example:

```text
Credit Limit
Available Limit
Card Limit
Spending Limit
```

While these terms may appear similar, they can have distinct meanings.

A standardized glossary enables AI systems to consistently map different expressions to the correct business concept.

---

# Core Product Terminology

---

## Credit Card

A revolving unsecured credit facility issued by the bank that allows customers to make purchases, withdraw cash (where permitted), and repay the borrowed amount according to the billing cycle.

---

## Credit Card Account

The underlying financial account that maintains:

- Credit limit
- Outstanding balance
- Statements
- Payments
- Rewards
- EMI plans
- Transaction history

The account exists independently of the physical card.

---

## Physical Card

The payment instrument issued to the customer that provides access to the Credit Card account.

---

## Virtual Card

A digital representation of the card used for online, tokenized, or digital transactions.

---

## Primary Card

The card issued to the principal account holder who is responsible for repayment obligations.

---

## Add-on Card

An additional card linked to the primary account that shares the same credit facility.

---

# Credit Terminology

---

## Credit Limit

The maximum amount of credit approved for a customer.

---

## Available Credit

The unused portion of the approved credit limit available for future transactions.

---

## Outstanding Balance

The total amount currently owed by the customer.

---

## Revolving Credit

A credit structure that allows customers to repeatedly borrow, repay, and reuse available credit without reapplying.

---

## Credit Utilisation

The percentage of the approved credit limit currently being used.

### Formula

```text
Outstanding Balance
------------------- × 100
Credit Limit
```

---

## Cash Advance

A facility allowing customers to withdraw cash against a portion of their approved credit limit.

---

# Billing Terminology

---

## Billing Cycle

The recurring period during which transactions are accumulated for statement generation.

---

## Statement

A periodic summary of account activity including:

- Transactions
- Payments
- Fees
- Interest
- Rewards
- Outstanding balance

---

## Statement Date

The date on which the statement is generated.

---

## Payment Due Date

The final date by which payment should be made for the billing cycle.

---

## Minimum Amount Due (MAD)

The minimum payment required to keep the account from being considered unpaid for the current cycle.

---

## Total Amount Due (TAD)

The full outstanding balance payable as of the statement date.

---

## Finance Charges

Interest charges applied to unpaid balances according to product terms.

---

## Interest-Free Period

The period during which eligible transactions may not attract interest if the statement balance is paid according to applicable conditions.

---

# Payment Terminology

---

## Full Payment

Repayment of the entire outstanding balance.

---

## Partial Payment

Repayment of an amount greater than the Minimum Amount Due but less than the Total Amount Due.

---

## AutoPay

An automated payment arrangement through which dues are debited from a designated account.

---

## Payment Allocation

The method by which received payments are applied to outstanding balances.

---

## Delinquency

A condition where required payments have not been made within the expected timeframe.

---

# Transaction Terminology

---

## Transaction

A financial event recorded against the card account.

Examples include:

- Purchases
- Cash withdrawals
- Refunds
- Fee postings

---

## Authorization

The process of validating whether a transaction may proceed.

---

## Settlement

The movement of funds between ecosystem participants after transaction authorization.

---

## Reversal

Cancellation of a previously authorized transaction.

---

## Refund

Return of funds for a completed transaction.

---

## Merchant

A business that accepts card payments for goods or services.

---

## Acquiring Bank

The financial institution that processes payments on behalf of merchants.
