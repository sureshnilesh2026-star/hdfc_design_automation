# Content & UX Writing Standards

Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

This document defines standards for product microcopy, in-app content, and UX writing across Mobile and Desktop experiences.

The goal is to ensure language is clear, consistent, human, and usable by people of varying literacy levels, reading abilities, and language backgrounds.

---

# Writing Principles

All product copy must be:

- Clear
- Concise
- Consistent
- Human
- Actionable
- Honest
- Inclusive

---

# Voice & Tone

Voice stays consistent across the product.

Tone adapts to context:

- Neutral for routine tasks
- Reassuring for errors or failures
- Celebratory for success, used sparingly
- Direct for warnings or irreversible actions

Never use humor in error states, financial actions, or security contexts.

---

# Terminology

Maintain a single approved term per concept.

Avoid:

- Synonyms for the same action (e.g. "Remove" vs "Delete" vs "Erase")
- Internal jargon or system/database field names
- Untranslated technical abbreviations

Maintain a shared glossary across product surfaces.

---

# Reading Level

Target a reading level accessible to a general audience.

Avoid:

- Compound/complex sentences
- Passive voice
- Idioms and culturally specific phrases

Prefer short sentences and common words over technical vocabulary.

---

# Buttons & CTAs

Buttons must:

- Start with a verb
- Describe the action, not the mechanism ("Save changes", not "Submit")
- Be unique when multiple buttons appear together
- Avoid vague labels like "OK", "Yes", "Submit" where a specific label is possible

---

# Empty States

Every empty state requires:

Explanation of why it's empty

Guidance on the next action

Avoid blank screens with no context.

---

# Error Messages

Error messages must:

Explain what went wrong in plain language

Explain how to recover

Avoid blaming the user

Avoid raw system/technical error codes without explanation

See also Error Handling in [[accessibility-standards]] and Error Recovery in [[ai-guardrails]].

---

# Confirmation & Irreversible Actions

Confirmation copy must:

State the action clearly

State the consequence, especially if irreversible

Avoid ambiguous wording like "Are you sure?" without context

---

# Notifications & Alerts

Notification copy must:

Lead with the most important information

Avoid unnecessary urgency or alarming language

Be scannable at a glance

---

# Localization Readiness

Avoid:

- Concatenated strings (building sentences from fragments)
- Culturally specific idioms, humor, or metaphors
- Hardcoded pluralization
- Text baked into images

Design copy to accommodate text expansion in translation (up to ~30%).

---

# Inclusive Language

Avoid:

- Gendered defaults where not required
- Ableist language ("crazy", "blind to", "crippled")
- Culturally exclusive references
- Assumptions about user ability, literacy, or access to technology
